Research and Projects

Research, applied projects, and open-source software by Shakil A. Rafi.

Most of what I work on sits somewhere between mathematics, machine learning, and a real problem someone needs solved. Here are some of the projects I’m proudest of.

Research

Classifying a poultry pathogen from its genome

With Cobb Genetics and the Goswami Lab · Frontiers in Microbiology, 2026

Enterococcus cecorum causes disease in poultry, but plenty of strains are harmless. We asked whether the way genes are arranged around genomic islands could tell the two apart. I engineered 84 genome-level features across 145 genomes and trained random forests with genome-grouped cross-validation so the model couldn’t cheat through near-duplicate samples. It reached an AUROC of 0.918 and beat the simpler baselines. When we held out whole sequencing projects, performance dropped to 0.694, which is an honest reminder that it needs prospective validation before anyone relies on it. Read the paper or the short write-up.

Omics and AI for neuronal gene expression

Brain Informatics, 2026

A review that follows computational neuro-omics from co-expression networks to graph neural networks and foundation models, with a focus on Alzheimer’s disease, schizophrenia, and epilepsy. The hard problems turn out to be less about models and more about harmonizing data, aligning spatial measurements, and getting causal interpretations right. Read the review

An algebra for neural networks

Ph.D. work · arXiv, 2024

My dissertation treated neural networks as algebraic objects you can add, compose, and scale, and used that to build networks that approximate solutions to the heat equation with explicit bounds on parameters, depth, and accuracy. Read the preprint

How researchers talk about AI in education

With researchers at Johns Hopkins University · under review

I built an LLM-assisted pipeline that classifies 12,000+ education research abstracts by their sentiment toward AI, using locally deployed models and checking them against human-labeled data. The goal is to see how that sentiment varies across countries and levels of economic development.

Interpretable text representations from questions

With researchers at Johns Hopkins University · ongoing, 2026

How many explicit questions does a document need? I encode a text as a language model’s answers to a bank of named questions, so every feature has a meaning I can read, and then test how far that list can shrink before accuracy drops. Early pilots reached 91.4% accuracy on SST-2 with 32 questions and 88.8% on Rotten Tomatoes with 64. I treat those as descriptive results, not proof that it transfers, and I’m keeping the negative results too. The code is private for now, but I’m happy to share it with reviewers who email me. Applied projects

Privacy-preserving transaction data with Mastercard

2025 – 2026

Real transaction data can’t go into a classroom. Working with Mastercard data scientists, I used Gaussian mixture models and their differentially private versions to generate synthetic transaction data that keeps the useful structure without exposing anyone. Students then used it for portfolio projects. Read the write-up or see the code.

Maternal health at Arkansas Blue Cross and Blue Shield

2023

I used difference-in-differences and Granger causality on claims data to study how policy affected maternal-health outcomes, built risk models from claims plus social determinants and Census data, and built an LSTM model that improved claims-volume forecasts by 25%. The results went to stakeholders as R Shiny dashboards.

Children’s nutrition in Birmingham, Alabama

With the UAB Heersink School of Medicine · 2024 – 2025

I analyzed pandemic-era survey data on the nutritional needs of children in underserved Birmingham neighborhoods using factor analysis and structural models. The findings were presented to the Alabama Department of Public Health.

Measuring a digital motivation course

With the Applied Sciences Performance Institute · 2024 – 2025

Together with a psychiatric team, I measured whether an online motivational course actually improved well-being (PERMA), motivation, depression, and anxiety. I compared intervention and control groups with ANOVA, ANCOVA, nonparametric tests, and A/B testing in R, and used the results to recommend which courses worked best for which groups.

Code

nnR

An R package on CRAN that implements the algebraic operations on neural networks from my dissertation. I wanted the math to be something you could run and poke at, not just read. It has full documentation, a vignette, and about 90% test coverage. Read the write-up.

A reimplementation of core FastQC functionality in Rust for sequencing quality control. Early in-memory benchmarks run more than 40 times faster than FastQC. Read the write-up.
